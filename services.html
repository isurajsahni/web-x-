What is the difference between UI/UX design and web design?

Web design covers the site people land on, the pages that explain what you do and ask for an enquiry. UI/UX design is for the thing they use after signing up: the dashboard, the settings screen, the signup flow with fifteen different states.

A lot of companies need both, and the two should feel like they belong together. We have separate pages for web design and UI/UX design, but it's the same team doing both. So the wording, the type and the components match, and your product doesn't look like someone else made it.

How long does a UI/UX design project take?

Something small, like one flow, an onboarding sequence or a pricing page, usually takes 1–2 weeks. A full marketing site is typically 2–4 weeks for design and build. A product interface with several user roles and lots of edge cases takes 4–10 weeks, depending on how many screens it needs.

Before we start you'll get a written milestone plan with the review dates fixed. Honestly, design is rarely what holds things up. Waiting for content and feedback is. If one person on your side can sign things off, everything moves quicker.

Do you work with our existing developers?

Often, yes. If you already have developers, we design around what they build with. We match their component library and name things the way their codebase does. They get files, prototypes and specs they can work straight from.

We also build, so if you'd rather give the whole thing to one team, there's no handoff step at all.

Can you redesign an existing site or product?

Yes, we do a lot of redesigns. The first job is finding where the current version loses people. Which screens get abandoned? Which features does nobody find? What does support keep getting asked?

Usually that makes the job smaller than people expect. If three screens are losing you conversions, we fix those three and leave the other forty alone.

How much does it cost?

We quote a fixed price and agree it with you before any work starts. We don't bill by the hour, and you won't get surprise change orders. The quote includes design, build, responsive testing and launch. Yearly costs like hosting, premium plugins and paid assets go on their own line.

The price mostly depends on scope. If you have a set budget, tell us on the first call and we'll plan the work to fit it.

What should we have ready before we start?

First, a call about what the product does and who uses it, and what you'd count as a good result. Then we'll ask for brand assets if you have them, any analytics or session recordings, and access to the current site or staging environment.

Missing some of that? Fine. No brand guidelines means we work from what you use now. And if there's no copy yet, we'll write it.
