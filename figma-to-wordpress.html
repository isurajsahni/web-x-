(02) The process

How the build works.

There are five stages. A staging site goes up early so you can watch it come together, and the timeline is fixed from the start.

Step 01

Send us the design

Share the Figma file and tell us what the site needs to do, and whether it connects to anything else. We’ll send a fixed quote and timeline within two business days.

Step 02

We build in WordPress

We build section by section on a clean, secure install, as a custom theme or a page-builder layout. Mobile is handled as we go, and you’ll get a staging link.

Step 03

Content and functionality

Custom fields, forms, blog or product structures and any integrations get wired up. Then we test every breakpoint, hover state and empty state.

Step 04

Review

Look at the staging site next to your Figma file and tell us what’s wrong. Two rounds of revisions are included.

Step 05

Move to live

We migrate the site to your host, connect the domain, and run final QA plus SEO and security checks. Then we publish and show your team around.

(03) Why lean

Why we keep WordPress lean.

A huge share of the web still runs on WordPress. It’s flexible, lots of people already know it, and you own everything, from the content to the hosting and the data. But a stretched marketplace theme with a dozen plugins can turn a good Figma design into a slow, fragile site. So we keep it lean, with a custom theme or a carefully set up page builder. You still get the freedom WordPress gives you, just without the weight.

A good test for any WordPress site: can your team update a page on a Friday afternoon without anything breaking?

(05) FAQs

Common WordPress questions

How long does Figma to WordPress conversion typically take?
A single landing page is usually 4-6 business days. For a 5-8 page business site with a CMS it’s typically 2-4 weeks, depending on how many unique templates there are, what functionality is needed and which integrations. We fix the timeline when we quote.
Do you build a custom theme or use a page builder?
Both. It depends on who’ll be maintaining the site. When performance and control matter most, we build a lightweight custom theme, often with ACF and the block editor. If your team wants to edit visually, we use Elementor or Gutenberg. We’ll tell you which we’d pick for your project and why.
Will the WordPress site look exactly like my Figma design?
Yes. We match spacing, type, colour and layout to your file at every breakpoint. If the design is missing hover states, mobile layouts or empty states, we’ll flag it and work out sensible defaults with you.
How much does Figma to WordPress cost?
We quote one fixed price for the build instead of an hourly rate. It’s driven by page count, how much custom theme and CMS functionality you need, and any third-party integrations. After a short scoping call you get an itemised quote and a timeline. Once you approve it, the price is fixed.
Is the WordPress site easy for my team to edit?
It should be. We set up custom fields and editable blocks so your team can update text, images and posts without touching code or breaking the design. There’s a short Loom walkthrough at handover.
What do I need to send you to get started?
Send the Figma link (view access is fine) and the fonts or font licences. Add final copy, or a note on what’s still placeholder, and your images and brand assets. We’ll also need hosting or WordPress admin access. Not designed mobile layouts, hover states or empty states yet? Tell us and we’ll propose defaults you can approve.
Is WordPress the right platform for my Figma design?
WordPress is a good fit for content-heavy sites and blogs, for teams already running it, and for projects that depend on particular plugins. For a design-led marketing site with visual editing, Figma to Webflow is often easier to manage. For a store, Figma to Shopify. For sites with a lot of motion, Figma to Framer.
