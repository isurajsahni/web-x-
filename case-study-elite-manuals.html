Elite Manuals

A 25,185-product store of heavy equipment manuals, with a structure that hid most of its catalogue from search engines and shoppers. We rebuilt how it is organised, described and crawled, without losing a single URL.

The challenge

  • Products that were hard to crawl. Visible pagination used ?product-page=N, and each of those pages declared page 1 as its canonical.
  • A flat catalogue. There were no pages for searches like "excavator service manual".
  • Near-duplicate product pages. Thousands of products shared a handful of description templates, with a second H1 on each page.
  • Weak schema and metadata. Product names and descriptions in the schema were templated, and regular pages were marked up as articles.
  • Heavy pages. Product pages downloaded cover images of up to 2.5 MB.
  • Hidden business problems. The contact form could not send messages, and custom code sat where the next theme update would erase it.

What we did

Technical SEO

Shop and category pagination moved to crawlable /page/N/ URLs, with 301 redirects that land on the same products in the same order. Pages past the end of a listing now return a real 404, and noindex pages left the sitemap. Custom code moved out of the parent theme into a must-use plugin, so updates cannot remove it.

Information architecture

We wrote a title parser that identifies brand, model, machine type, manual type and serial edition for each product, then built 149 new categories on three patterns. Products were assigned additively. Because product URLs do not include the category, the new structure needed no product redirects at all.

On-page SEO at catalogue scale

Product descriptions were generated from each product's own data, with links to other manuals for the same model and guidance on matching serial numbers. Category pages gained their own H1, intro, guide text and metadata. The original descriptions stayed stored and restorable.

Structured data, AEO and GEO

We cleaned up Product schema, removed incorrect Article and Person markup, and added FAQ schema and a support contact point. Direct question-and-answer blocks and plain factual statements make the business easy for answer engines and AI tools to read, and real figures now sit in the page HTML rather than in counters that start at zero.

Brand-safe content

Brand names are used only to identify the equipment a manual applies to, in original copy with no "genuine", "official" or "OEM" claims. A non-affiliation notice sits on product and category pages and on Home, About and Contact.

Redesign and performance

Home, About, Contact and the Shop and category template were redesigned, with a sticky category filter, new review cards and a discount badge showing the real saving. Every image upload gained a WebP copy, served automatically, with originals kept and new uploads converted on upload.

Key decisions

DecisionWhy it mattered
Additive category changesThe new structure could be added without a single redirect for products.
Repeatable deploymentImporting the local database would have wiped live orders and customers, so database changes were written as scripts that can run safely on the live site.
Editable pages over code-rendered pagesThe first redesign rendered pages through shortcodes, which looked right but could not be edited in Elementor. We rebuilt it with standard widgets, so the client's team can change any text, link or image themselves.
