How long does website development take?

“When will it be live?” is the question every client asks. Here are realistic 2026 timelines for every type of website - and the honest truth about what actually speeds a project up or drags it out (hint: it’s usually not the code).

By Web{X} Studio·Updated July 2026·7 min read

“How long does website development take?” sounds like it should have a simple answer, and in a way it does - as long as you know what kind of website you’re building. A one-page campaign site and a custom SaaS platform are separated by months. Here are the real ranges, and what moves them.

At Web{X} Studio we ship projects of every size. These are the timelines we’d quote you honestly.

The short answer

Landing page: 1-2 weeks. Business website: 3-6 weeks. Ecommerce store: 6-10 weeks. Custom web app: 3-6 months+. The biggest variable isn’t the developer - it’s how fast you supply content and feedback.

Realistic timelines by type

TypeTypical timelineWhat drives it
Landing page1-2 weeksOne page, tight scope
Business website (5-12 pages)3-6 weeksDesign rounds, content, pages
Ecommerce store6-10 weeksCatalogue, payments, shipping
Custom web app / SaaS3-6 months+Features, back-end, testing

These assume a professional studio and a reasonably responsive client. The website development process - discovery, design, build, content, testing, launch - runs through all of them; bigger projects simply spend longer in each stage.

What speeds a project up

  • Content ready upfront. Final copy and images on day one is the biggest accelerator there is.
  • Tight, clear scope. Knowing exactly what you’re building prevents mid-project detours.
  • Fast feedback. Approvals in a day, not a fortnight, keep momentum.
  • A no-code platform. Tools like Webflow can shave weeks off a marketing-site build.
  • One decision-maker. Design-by-committee is the quiet killer of timelines.

What slows a project down

Almost every delayed website traces back to the same handful of causes - and notice that coding is rarely one of them:

  • Unready content. The number-one reason launches slip. A finished design with placeholder text can’t go live.
  • Scope creep. “Can we also add…” is how a 4-week project becomes a 4-month one.
  • Slow approvals. Every day a decision waits, the timeline waits with it.
  • Complex integrations. Payments, CRMs and custom APIs add real, unavoidable time.
A website ships fast when the brief is clear and the content is ready - the code is rarely the bottleneck.

How to plan your launch

Work backwards from your target date and build in buffer. If you need to launch in six weeks, your content should be ready in week one, not week five. Agree the scope, name a single decision-maker, and keep feedback quick. Do that, and even an ambitious build stays on schedule.
